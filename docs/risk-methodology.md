# Risk scoring and treatment

Scores are fictional qualitative prioritization aids, not loss estimates. Review assumptions with business owners.
Inherent risk is before controls; residual risk is **current** exposure after evidenced existing controls, not the hoped-for result of an unfinished plan.

| Rating | Likelihood in the planning horizon | Impact on scoped service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rare; strong barriers and little credible exposure | Negligible disruption; no material data impact |
| 2 | Unlikely; limited credible exposure | Minor, quickly recoverable disruption |
| 3 | Possible; plausible exposure and incomplete barriers | Material team disruption or limited sensitive-data exposure |
| 4 | Likely; significant exposure or repeated precursors | Major service disruption or significant sensitive-data compromise |
| 5 | Almost certain; active exposure or repeated exploitation | Severe or sustained disruption, widespread compromise or major contractual impact |

Score = likelihood × impact. Low: 1–4; Medium: 5–9; High: 10–16; Critical: 17–25.
Same bands apply to inherent and residual scores. Existing detection can reduce exposure only where the owner can
justify the effect; it does not automatically reduce impact. The sample RSK-001 keeps its residual score at 20 because removal is unverified.

Treat by avoiding, mitigating, transferring/sharing or accepting. For each treatment record owner, action, deadline,
dependency and status. Transfer does not remove accountability. No sample risk is accepted. Illustrative authority:
Low/Medium by service owner; High by security and business executives; Critical by executive risk committee.
Acceptance must include rationale, expiry and monitoring; legal and contractual obligations cannot be waived by a score.
Reassess after verified treatment, material change, incident or at least quarterly. Overdue high risks escalate weekly.
